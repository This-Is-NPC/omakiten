The Five W Two H frame (a staple of quality-management problem definition, Ishikawa lineage) is a checklist for completeness. Walk all seven prompts before treating a request as understood; a missing answer is a gap to close, not a detail to infer.

- **What** — the concrete change or deliverable. State it as an observable outcome, not an activity.
- **Why** — the driving need. If the why is weak, the work is a candidate for cutting.
- **Who** — who requests it, who consumes it, who signs off. Name the accountable party.
- **When** — deadline, dependency window, or "no fixed date". Distinguish a hard constraint from a preference.
- **Where** — the system, module, environment, or surface the change lands in.
- **How** — the proposed approach or method, if one is already implied.
- **How much** — scope, effort, or budget. The quantitative bound that separates this iteration from the next.

## Discipline

Do not paper over a vague answer. "Make it faster" is incomplete until *how much* faster and *where* are pinned. When the requester cannot answer a prompt, record the gap explicitly and flag it as a risk rather than guessing a value.

## Boundaries

This is an elicitation lens, not a document format. Feed the answers into the requirements, acceptance-criteria, or planning artifact that owns the structure. Use it whenever a request arrives underspecified.
