SMART (Doran, 1981, *Management Review*) is a five-test checklist that converts a vague intent into a goal you can verify. Run a goal statement against each letter; a goal that fails a letter cannot be objectively passed or failed later.

- **Specific** — names the precise outcome and the part of the system it affects. "Improve performance" is not specific; "reduce checkout p95 latency" is.
- **Measurable** — carries a number and a measurement method. If you cannot state how the result will be measured, you cannot tell when it is reached.
- **Achievable** — within the reach of the available capacity and constraints. A goal known to be impossible is demoralising rather than directional.
- **Relevant** — connected to a real need or higher objective. A measurable, achievable goal that nobody needs is wasted effort.
- **Time-bound** — has a deadline or window. Without a horizon, "Measurable" never triggers a verdict.

## Using it

Phrase the goal as a single sentence and confirm all five letters hold before committing to it. The output is a success signal a tester or reviewer could check, not an aspiration. Where a goal touches delivery, fold its measurable/time-bound parts directly into acceptance criteria.

## Boundaries

SMART sharpens an individual goal; it does not rank competing goals or decompose a large one into deliverables. Pair it with prioritisation for ordering and with story-writing for breakdown.
